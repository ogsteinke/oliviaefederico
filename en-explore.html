Exploring Arona

The reason we chose Arona: a long walk right on the water, lined with cafés, restaurants and shops, with the Rocca di Angera just across the lake. One street back are Corso Cavour, the pedestrian shopping street, and Piazza del Popolo.

The Borromean Islands

Isola Bella, with the Borromeo palace and its terraced baroque gardens; Isola dei Pescatori, a fishing village with restaurants on the water; and Isola Madre with its botanical garden. Ferries go from Arona's pier, or more quickly from Stresa.

Lago Maggiore Ferry

Hop on a ferry at Arona's pier and let the lake show you around. Get off in Stresa, a Belle Époque town of grand old lakefront hotels and gardens, or simply stay on board and enjoy the sights from the water.

Map · Ferries ↗

Santa Caterina del Sasso

A monastery built into the cliffs above the lake on the Lombard shore. Legend says a merchant, Alberto Besozzi, survived a storm here in the 12th century and spent the rest of his life as a hermit on this rock. Arrive by boat for the best view, or walk down 268 steps (or take the lift) from the road above to its frescoed church and portico.

Rocca di Angera

A medieval fortress on a rocky spur above Angera with views over the whole lake, owned by the Borromeo family since 1449.
